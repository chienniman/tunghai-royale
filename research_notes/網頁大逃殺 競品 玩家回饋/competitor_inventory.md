# Competitor inventory: browser-based (no-install) battle royale and shooter games — state as of 12 November 2026 (網頁版大逃殺／射擊遊戲競品盤點)

Reading notes that apply to every section:
- All pages were fetched on 2026-11-12. "Semrush" figures are third-party *estimates* of visits to a game's **own domain** only; play that happens inside portals (Poki, CrazyGames) is not counted, so portal-first games (Venge.io, Narrow.One, Hazmob FPS, Fortzone) are under-represented in the traffic table.
- "CG likes" = like / dislike counters shown on the CrazyGames game page; "Poki votes" = vote count on the Poki game page. Both are lifetime counters and are the only like-for-like popularity measure available across portal games.
- Items marked **(snippet)** come from a search-engine summary of the page; the page itself was not opened or returned HTTP 403. Treat them as lower confidence.
- Only one short direct quotation is used in this file (Jason Kapalka, in section 4); everything else is paraphrased.

## 1. Which 3D browser shooters / battle royales matter today? (哪些 3D 網頁射擊／大逃殺現在重要？)

### Takeaway
Measured by own-domain traffic in Sep–Oct 2026, the largest browser shooters are Deadshot.io (~7.1M visits/month, mostly Bangladesh and India), Krunker (~3.5M) and Shell Shockers (~2.8M); on the portals the largest are Hazmob FPS, Shell Shockers, Veck.io, Bullet Force and Fortzone Battle Royale (CrazyGames) and Venge.io (Poki, 2.3M votes). Genuine 3D battle-royale modes in a browser are rare (Fortzone Battle Royale, Voxiom.io, War Brokers, 1v1.LOL); most "battle royale" portal traffic goes to arena shooters with short rounds, and Mini Royale: Nations appears to be offline.

### Inferences
- The competitive set for a new browser battle royale is not "other browser battle royales" (there are very few in 3D) but short-round arena shooters and Unity ports that already own the portal front pages; a newcomer competes for the same player minutes.
- South Asia (Bangladesh, India) is now the largest audience for several browser shooters (Deadshot.io, Kirka.io, CrazyGames as a whole); the US school/Chromebook audience still dominates Shell Shockers, Krunker, Kour.io and the 2D battle royales. No major title shows Taiwan in its top-5 countries, so a Taiwan-campus game would be competing for attention mostly through local novelty rather than against an entrenched local favourite.
- The fastest-growing entrants of 2024–2026 (Hazmob FPS, Fortzone, Veck.io) reached top-tier like counts within 1–2 years, so the portal market is still open to new shooters.

## 2. Which top-down / 2D .io battle royales matter, and what do they show about what players accept visually? (俯視 2D .io 大逃殺)

### Takeaway
The 2D battle-royale niche is alive and has consolidated around surviv.io and its descendants: the fan remake survev.io (~1.7M visits in Oct 2026) now out-draws both ZombsRoyale.io (~0.66M) and Kongregate's official surviv.io relaunch of March 2026 (~0.5M). These games prove that players accept extremely minimal visuals (a player is literally a circle with two smaller circles) when rounds start instantly, last about five minutes and the battle-royale loop is complete.

### Cited Findings
- surviv.io: created by Justin Kim and Nick Clark; browser release 11 Oct 2017; Android Nov 2018; Steam Sep 2020; Kongregate acquired it in Dec 2019; Kongregate announced the sunset on 13 Feb 2023 and shut it down on 2 Mar 2023, naming Bit Heroes Arena as successor; the game was relaunched on Kongregate on 20 Mar 2026 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surviv.io)
- The surviv.io site is live again (version 0.9.3) with a "welcome back" post dated 18 Mar 2026 and a 10 Apr 2026 post announcing the return of its Survivr pass; it shows ad-blocker requests and Kongregate links — [surviv.io](https://surviv.io/)
- Kongregate lists surviv.io in its "Hot" games with a 4.4-star rating and 208.6K plays — [Kongregate](https://www.kongregate.com/en/games/kongregate/surviv-io)
- surviv.io traffic: ~505K visits in Sep 2026 (US 41.2%, Germany 5.2%, Peru 5.2%, Japan 4.4%) — [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/website/surviv.io/overview/)
- survev.io is an open-source recreation of surviv.io **(snippet of the repository description)** — [GitHub survev](https://github.com/leia-uwu/survev); it drew ~1.73M visits in Oct 2026 (964K in Sep), 64% from the US — [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/website/survev.io/overview/)
- Suroi: open-source (GPL-3.0) 2D battle royale inspired by surviv.io; TypeScript, Bun, PixiJS, Vite, uWebSockets.js; 518 stars, 244 forks, 8,449 commits — [GitHub](https://github.com/HasangerGames/suroi); site at v0.30.5 with weekly "Suroi Sundays" limited-time events through Aug 2026 and a repeatedly delayed UI overhaul; funded via Ko-fi; has a QQ group — [suroi.io](https://suroi.io/); ~57K visits in Sep 2026 (US 59%, Japan 8.9%, Russia 8.1%) — [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/website/suroi.io/overview/)
- ZombsRoyale.io: End Game Interactive (Bellevue, Washington); released 14 Mar 2018; a team of about three built the first version in four weeks; the CEO, Yang C. Liu, led development and did the art; iOS/Android in May 2018, PC client later; ~45M players by 2020 and 120M+ downloads/unique players as of 2024; $3M seed round in 2020 (investors include Supercell); up to 100 players per match (some bots), ~5-minute matches; simple 2D top-down graphics on the same engine as Zombs.io — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZombsRoyale.io)
- ZombsRoyale.io traffic: ~656K visits in Oct 2026 (US 67.7%) — [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/website/zombsroyale.io/overview/); CrazyGames calls it a top-rated .io game in the genre — [CrazyGames](https://www.crazygames.com/t/battle-royale)
- BuildRoyale.io: ~42K visits in Sep 2026 — [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/website/buildroyale.io/overview/)
- What reviewers said about the look: the Taiwanese outlet T客邦 described surviv.io's character as one big circle plus two small circles for fists, viewed top-down, and argued it works because all the classic battle-royale items are kept, players can hide in buildings and under trees, a round is under five minutes and no gaming hardware is needed — [T客邦, 21 Mar 2018](https://www.techbang.com/posts/57380-2d-jedi-survive-without-professional-graphics-cards-can-also-be-a-solution-to-eat-chicken-addiction)
- VG247 (May 2018) called surviv.io one of the most popular battle royale titles after Fortnite and PUBG; PC Gamer praised its pace and short matchmaking; its designer said the goal was to minimise time between matches — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surviv.io)
- A June 2026 "best browser shooters" list still includes Survev.io and Zombs Royale alongside Krunker and Shell Shockers — [dinogame.gg, 2 Jun 2026](https://dinogame.gg/blog/best-browser-shooter-games/)
- On Poki, the "battle royale" category is mostly non-shooter last-one-standing games (snakes, demolition derby, chickens) — [Poki](https://poki.com/en/battle-royale)

### Inferences
- Minimal, abstract 2D art is fully accepted when it is *consistent and readable*; the surviv.io lineage has kept the same circle-and-fists look for nine years and fans rebuilt it twice (survev, Suroi) rather than "improve" it. Ugly-because-inconsistent is the problem, not low fidelity.
- The official owner shutting the game down (2023) and fans keeping it alive, to the point that the fan remake has ~3x the traffic of the official relaunch, shows the loop matters more than the brand or art.
- A top-down or strongly stylised presentation is a legitimate way to sidestep 3D asset quality entirely; a one-person art effort (ZombsRoyale's CEO did the art) can carry a 100M+ player game in 2D.

### Gaps
- Engine for surviv.io and ZombsRoyale.io is not stated in the sources found (Suroi uses PixiJS).
- Monetisation details for ZombsRoyale.io are not covered in its Wikipedia article; no 2025–2026 status statement from End Game Interactive was found (traffic shows it is still live).
- How Suroi/survev art is produced (SVG, community contributions) is not documented on the pages fetched.

## 3. For each notable game: release year, developer, current status, public numbers, platforms, monetisation (各遊戲基本資料)

### Takeaway
Most of the notable games are 5–9 years old, made by teams of one to a handful of people, and still running in November 2026; the clear casualties are crypto-tied Mini Royale: Nations (apparently offline) and Krunker's Steam version (delisted), while surviv.io went the other way and was revived in 2026. Hard public numbers exist only for a few: Shell Shockers (200M+ lifetime players, 300–350K DAU peaks in 2022, low-seven-figure USD annual revenue, 80–90% from ads), Krunker (200M+ players by 2022), ZombsRoyale (120M+ by 2024) and Venge/Narrow.One (50M+ Poki gameplays each by 2022).

### Cited Findings

**Krunker.io**
- Started in 2018 by Swiss developer Sidney de Vries; FRVR announced its acquisition on 12 May 2022 (price undisclosed); at that time: 200M+ unique players, 12 rotating maps, 50,000 custom maps and modes, full modding and mapping tools, and a player economy with thousands of tradeable skins; the founder said the team would keep creative control — [Game Developer press release, 12 May 2022](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/press-release/frvr-acquires-popular-free-to-play-shooter-krunkerio)
- Steam: released 1 Feb 2021, developer and publisher shown as FRVR; the page now carries a notice that the game is no longer available on the Steam store; all-time reviews Mostly Positive (78% of 15,152 English reviews) — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1408720/Krunker/); players on the Steam forum reported it vanished without explanation **(snippet)** — [Steam community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1408720/discussions/0/4041482690812466335)
- Steam concurrency is tiny: about 34 online with an all-time peak of 816 per Tracker.gg; Steambase shows 15 concurrent (page dated 4 Oct 2026) and a different peak (473, June 2023) **(snippets; the two trackers conflict)** — [Tracker.gg](https://tracker.gg/population/steam/1408720), [Steambase](https://steambase.io/games/krunker)
- A "Save Krunker" petition (started 17 Jan 2024, 58 signatures) claims that after the FRVR acquisition the game suffered heavy monetisation and community neglect and that players and staff left — [Change.org](https://www.change.org/p/save-krunker)
- Still playable in browser on krunker.io / FRVR, described by FRVR as a pixelated first-person shooter with community-made maps — [FRVR](https://frvr.com/play/krunker/); ~3.5M visits in Oct 2026 — [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/website/krunker.io/overview/)

## 4. Engine / rendering tech, how assets are made, and how size and frame rate are kept acceptable (引擎、素材製作方式、容量與效能)

### Takeaway
Engines split into two camps: JavaScript engines (PlayCanvas for Venge.io and MiniRoyale2, Babylon.js for Shell Shockers and Bloxd.io, three.js for Narrow.One and — by community attribution — Krunker) and Unity WebGL (Bullet Force, Forward Assault Remix, Hazmob FPS, Fortzone, 1v1.LOL, War Brokers, Rush Team). Wherever asset production is documented, it is hand-authored, deliberately simple stylised art made in standard tools (Blender, Substance, Photoshop/Illustrator) by one or two in-house artists, with a theme chosen to make simplicity look intentional (eggs, voxels, pixel textures, medieval archers); no source describes procedurally generated art as the shipped look of a successful title.

### Inferences
- The common pattern is "pick a style in which cheap assets look deliberate": eggs (no limbs, no faces, no animation rig to speak of), voxels (art = data), pixelated low-res textures on boxy geometry (Krunker), flat low-poly with one artist's consistent hand (Venge, Narrow.One). None of the successful titles tries for realism in a JavaScript engine; the realistic-military look is left to Unity ports from mobile, and reviewers call those generic.
- A single dedicated artist (or artist-developer) using Blender is enough for a top-tier browser shooter; what matters is that one person or one style guide governs everything.
- Community content (maps, skins) is how the longest-lived title (Krunker) scaled its asset volume without a large art team.
- JavaScript-engine games mostly reach phones and tablets, while several of the big Unity WebGL shooters are desktop-only on portals (with exceptions both ways). For a three.js project, mobile-browser reach is an existing advantage worth protecting when upgrading visuals.
- Large open battle-royale maps are a known performance hazard in the browser: the one developer who documented trying it (MiniRoyale2) reported performance problems and thin lobbies, then succeeded with small arenas.

### Gaps
- Engines of Krunker (first-party confirmation), Deadshot.io, Kirka.io, Voxiom.io, Ev.io, Veck.io, Kour.io and surviv.io could not be confirmed from public sources; voxiom.io returned HTTP 403 and kirka.io / deadshot.io returned only a title.
- No statement from any of these developers about using (or not using) purchased asset-store packs was found; the generic-assets observation comes from a reviewer, not from the developers.
- No first-party statements were found on texture budgets, polygon budgets, compression (e.g. KTX2/Draco) or lightmapping for any of these games.
- Whether Narrow.One has since moved off three.js to the developer's own engine is unconfirmed.

## 5. Which are regarded as the best-looking browser shooters, and what makes them look good? (公認畫面最好的是哪些？為什麼？)

### Takeaway
There is no authoritative ranking; the available 2026 commentary consists of listicles that mostly describe style rather than judge it. Within that, Venge.io is the title singled out for a clean, coherent low-poly look, Bullet Force is described as a conventional military (Call of Duty-like) presentation with heavier hardware needs, Narrow.One and Ev.io are noted for a consistent stylised theme, and the explicit criticism is aimed at Unity ports whose art looks generic.

### Cited Findings
- Of ten browser shooters in a June 2026 roundup, Venge.io is the only one praised specifically for its art (a tidy low-poly style that is light on hardware); Krunker is praised for feel (blocky Quake-like arenas, sharp hit feedback) and called the leading browser FPS; War Brokers is described as clearly not AAA fidelity but fully featured — [dinogame.gg, 2 Jun 2026](https://dinogame.gg/blog/best-browser-shooter-games/)
- A "15 best browser FPS in 2026" list ranks on access, responsiveness and mode variety, not visuals; it describes Ev.io as clean with futuristic arenas, Bullet Force as conventional military, Kirka.io as blocky/voxel, Pixel Warfare as Minecraft-like old-school, and Shell Shockers as egg-themed, casual and family-friendly — [itechguides, 16 Aug 2026](https://www.itechguides.com/?p=401851)
- TheGamer's launch coverage called Ev.io's low-poly, Halo-influenced sci-fi look easy on the eyes while staying GPU-friendly — [TheGamer, 20 Jan 2021](https://www.thegamer.com/browser-based-fps-ev-io-low-end-machines/)
- A Playgama roundup marks down Hazmob FPS, MFPS: Military Combat and Special Ops: GO for low visual fidelity and generic assets, and praises games with a bright, toy-like or paint-splash identity — [Playgama, Apr/Oct 2026](https://playgama.com/blog/?p=13716); CrazyGames' own page, by contrast, describes Hazmob's graphics as excellent and clean — [CrazyGames](https://www.crazygames.com/game/hazmob-fps-online-shooter)
- A 13-game browser-FPS list (2024, updated 7 May 2026) says almost nothing about graphics for Krunker, Bullet Force, Forward Assault Remix, Rush Team or Shell Shockers (only that the last is egg-themed and cartoonish); the one game it calls visually impressive is Fortnite played through cloud gaming, i.e. not a native web game — [Wave Browser blog](https://wavebrowser.co/blog/fps-browser-games)
- Player comments on Venge.io's itch.io page call the graphics good and the game beautiful — [itch.io](https://onrush.itch.io/vengeio)
- Narrow.One's Steam tags are Stylized, Colorful and Cartoon; its maps are themed sets (samurai castles, desert oases, Viking forts, Greek sky temples) with alternate versions and changing weather — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3788170)
- On the three.js forum a developer noted Krunker's very sharp shadows as a distinctive rendering trait — [three.js forum, 14 Jun 2019](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/what-are-other-awesome-first-person-games-made-with-three-js-besides-gorescript-and-xibalba/1655)
- Venge.io's presentation layer includes hero characters with abilities, weapon effects, sound effects and voice lines, modelled on Overwatch and Paladins — [Poki interview](https://poki.com/blog/meet-onrush-studio)

### Inferences
- What reads as "good-looking" in a browser shooter is coherence, not polygon count: one palette and one level of detail across characters, weapons and maps (Venge, Narrow.One, Ev.io), a strong theme per map (Narrow.One's castles and temples; Venge's desert / temple / tundra / town maps), and crisp readable lighting (hard shadows, clean silhouettes).
- "Generic" is the main visual criticism reviewers make, and it is aimed at mixed realistic asset packs; a distinctive identity (eggs, archers, paint, toys) earns praise even at very low fidelity.
- Audio-visual feedback (hit feedback, weapon effects, voice lines) and UI polish are repeatedly cited alongside art as what makes a game feel finished — these are cheaper to improve than a full asset replacement.
- Realistic fidelity (Bullet Force) is achievable in the browser only via Unity WebGL and costs hardware reach; it is not the route the JavaScript-engine leaders take.

### Gaps
- No Reddit, YouTube or forum consensus threads on "best-looking browser FPS" could be retrieved; the evidence is listicles and store metadata, so "generally regarded" is weakly supported.
- An unpinned 2026 listicle (seen only in a search summary) reportedly calls Venge.io arguably the best-looking browser FPS and says it is built on three.js with custom shaders; the page could not be identified and the engine claim contradicts the developer's own statement (PlayCanvas).
- Search summaries also reported listicles calling Bullet Force the most visually polished / console-like browser FPS (high-fidelity weapon models, realistic environments) and Forward Assault Remix notable for advanced graphics, but the specific pages could not be pinned down or opened (one candidate returned HTTP 403, another did not contain the claim), so these are unverified.
- FRVR's own "Top 10 Browser FPS Games in 2026" post returned HTTP 410 and could not be read.
- No source analyses specific techniques (lightmaps, post-processing, animation quality) per game.

## 6. Battle royale or shooter games set on a real university campus or other recognisable real-world location, especially in Taiwan, China, Japan, Korea (真實校園／真實地點的射擊或大逃殺案例)

### Takeaway
No browser battle royale set on a real East Asian university campus was found; the nearest precedents are institution-sanctioned shooter maps built inside Fortnite (Tokyo University of Technology / Nihon Kogakuin's campus gun-game, March 2025; University of Staffordshire's StaffsVerse, January 2025), a Korean student-made zombie shooter on the Korea Aerospace University campus, and many Minecraft campus recreations including NTU's and NTUST's in Taiwan in 2021. Reception splits sharply by framing: sanctioned or celebratory campus recreations earned positive press, while unsanctioned student-made Counter-Strike maps of real schools in North America (2007, 2013) triggered discipline, police involvement and local outrage.

### Cited Findings

**Japan**
- Katayanagi Gakuen released a Fortnite map recreating the Hachioji campus of Tokyo University of Technology / Nihon Kogakuin on 7 Mar 2025: a gun-game for up to 6 players (first to 20 eliminations), flown between stages by helicopter; stages recreate the research building's first floor and lobby, the Central Plaza and (from late April 2025) the library lounge, the last made with students of the CG department; island code 2766-7751-0142. The real campus is about 380,000 m², but only a few landmark spaces were built — [gamebiz.jp, 10 Mar 2025](https://gamebiz.jp/news/402119)
- A virtual Shinjuku (around the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building) was generated from satellite data with AI by entrepreneur Kou Sato and published in Fortnite in Aug 2023 with sightseeing, building, battle and driving; the reviewer praised the accuracy of the recreation; no play numbers reported — [MoguLive, 25 Aug 2023](https://www.moguravr.com/?p=234344)
- A Waseda doctoral student recreated all of Tokyo in Minecraft (reported Mar 2026) **(snippet)** — [unwire.hk, 18 Mar 2026](https://unwire.hk/2026/03/18/tokyo-minecraft-plateau-waseda-phd-recreation/game-channel/)

**Taiwan**
- National Taiwan University's online graduation on 26 Jun 2021 opened with a virtual campus tour made by the student group NTU Minecraft, showing graduates walking from the lawn by the Main Library along Royal Palm Boulevard past the Fu Bell and the main gate; the university described the creative segments as memorable — [NTU official news, 14 Jul 2021](https://www.ntu.edu.tw/english/spotlight/2021/1966_20210714.html)
- 天下雜誌 reported that the group rebuilt the NTU campus at 1:1 in Minecraft and that close to ten thousand people watched its "Minecraft graduation" stream **(snippet; page returned 403)** — [CommonWealth Magazine](https://www.cw.com.tw/article/5117274)
- NTUST: eight computer-science students rebuilt the campus in Minecraft in about a month and a half (nearly 20 buildings, one block = 50 cm); the sailboat-shaped building was the hardest because of its curves; clubs recruited inside it and tours and scavenger hunts for new students were planned — [Taiwan News, 28 Sep 2021](https://www.taiwannews.com.tw/en/news/4299644)
- A student-initiated Minecraft rebuild of the NYCU campus with alumni-association support was announced in April 2026 **(snippet; press-release platform, not editorial)** — [CNA press-release service](https://cna.com.tw/postwrite/eng/429694)

**Korea**
- "새내기의 모험 (Adventure of Freshman)" by the student group ZKAU is a free 3D zombie-shooting and campus-tour game for PC set on the Korea Aerospace University campus, with first/third-person views, three weapons, a shop and a boss; 152 MB download; tagged campus, metaverse, shooting, tour; the page shows no ratings or comments; the developer says more campuses could be added through partnerships with other schools — [itch.io](https://sunnyside-up0102.itch.io/adventure-of-freshman)
- A Seoil University student made a first-person Unity campus exploration for freshmen and visitors, with no combat **(snippet)** — [itch.io](https://soda3641.itch.io/test1)

**China**
- "Chinese Universities" (TitanForge, 26 Dec 2024, free on Steam): a sandbox that recreates a Chinese university campus (sports fields, dormitories, teaching buildings, library) explorable on foot or by vehicle; no specific university named; 86% positive from 22 reviews — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3321490)
- "Return to campus" (developer 聂宇星, 12 Jan 2025, free on Steam, Unreal Engine 5): an open-world school sandbox with first-person and gun content in an apparently fictional school; 76% positive from 1,785 reviews, recent reviews mixed — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3174480)
- Game for Peace (和平精英)'s user-creation mode 綠洲啟元 hosts more than 150,000 user/workshop maps and modes and passed 33M daily active users; campus e-sports events are run with universities, but no campus-recreation map was found **(snippets)** — [IT之家](https://www.ithome.com/0/888/451.htm), [揚子晚報](https://www.yzwb.net/news/ty/202506/t20250624_226560.html)

**Outside East Asia (for reception)**
- University of Staffordshire launched "StaffsVerse" (13 Jan 2025), described as the UK's first university campus recreated in Fortnite: three campuses built by games students in Unreal Engine, with mini-games; the vice-chancellor publicly praised it — [Greater Birmingham Chambers of Commerce](https://www.greaterbirminghamchambers.com/resource/game-changing-virtual-campus-is-recreated-in-fortnite-game.html)
- Texas, 2007: a 17-year-old at Clements High School (Fort Bend ISD) made and shared a Counter-Strike map of his school shortly after the Virginia Tech shooting; after a parent complaint he was questioned and frisked, police searched his bedroom and took five decorative swords, no crime was found and no charges were laid, but he was transferred to an alternative education centre; the local Chinese community protested and about 120 people attended a board meeting that collapsed for lack of quorum — [The Register, 4 May 2007](https://www.theregister.com/2007/05/03/student_counterstrike_map_texasschool/)
- British Columbia, 2013: a Counter-Strike map of Port Moody Secondary School drew negative local reaction when a video surfaced; the creator said there was no malicious intent **(snippets; pages returned 403)** — [Techdirt, 29 Mar 2013](https://www.techdirt.com/2013/03/29/counter-strike-map-school-causes-outrage/), [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/shocker-counter-strike-map-based-on-local-school-cause-458661483)
- US universities' student Minecraft campus builds during 2020 (UPenn, UCSB, Brown, RIT and others) received favourable university and press coverage; UPenn students floated Hunger Games-style contests between schools **(snippet)** — [Yahoo Finance / Business Insider](https://money.yahoo.com/upenn-students-recreated-campus-minecraft-114200557.html)

**Small three.js battle royales and real-world map tech (closest technical peers)**
- "Survive the Horde" (posted 31 Oct 2026): an independent three.js browser FPS with zombie survival, co-op by room code and a battle-royale mode with loot and a shrinking zone, set in an Indian-village-inspired environment; the developer asked for feedback on environment, rendering and performance, but the only replies concerned netcode (server authority, interpolation, lag compensation) — [three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/survive-the-horde-a-multiplayer-zombie-survival-battle-royale-game-built-with-three-js/96185)
- The building blocks for real-world geometry in three.js exist (three.js ships a 3D Tiles loader example; Google offers Photorealistic 3D Tiles), but no browser battle royale built on a real city or campus with them was found — [three.js example](https://threejs.org/examples/webgl_loader_3dtiles.html), [Google Maps Platform docs](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/tile/3d-tiles)

### Inferences
- A browser battle royale on a real Taiwanese campus appears to have no direct precedent; the recognisable-place appeal is real (NTU and NTUST Minecraft builds drew press and thousands of viewers), but every East Asian precedent that got attention was built on an existing platform (Fortnite, Minecraft) and so inherited that platform's art quality instead of solving the asset problem itself.
- The Japanese campus gun-game is the most instructive model: it did not attempt the whole 38-hectare campus but three or four landmark spaces at high fidelity, co-produced with students, in a gun-game (arena) format rather than a full battle royale. Scope reduction to a few instantly recognisable landmarks is how a real campus was made to look good.
- What made the Taiwanese Minecraft campuses land was landmark recognition (Royal Palm Boulevard, Fu Bell, NTUST's sailboat building) and a communal occasion (graduation, freshman tours) — not combat.
- Framing and sanction matter for reception. Institution-backed or celebratory projects were praised; unsanctioned "shoot-up-my-school" maps drew punishment in North America. East Asia has a different context from the US on school shootings, but a campus shooter is still safer with non-realistic violence (the Shell Shockers logic: stylised characters make gunplay read as play) and, ideally, some form of student-club or university goodwill.
- The Korean student project shows the typical fate of a self-built campus shooter with its own assets: a downloadable curiosity with no visible ratings or comments.

### Gaps
- No play counts or reception data were found for the Tokyo University of Technology Fortnite map, StaffsVerse or the Korean campus game.
- Chinese-language community sources (巴哈姆特, PTT, Dcard, Bilibili, 知乎) returned nothing relevant through the available search tool, which is US-centric; student-made campus maps for CS / PUBG-style games in Taiwan or China very likely exist (Bilibili videos, club projects) but could not be surfaced or verified. A manual search on those sites is recommended.
- Whether any university in Taiwan has objected to, or endorsed, a shooter set on its campus is unknown.
- The outcome of the Port Moody (2013) case and the final appeal outcome in the Texas (2007) case were not verified.
